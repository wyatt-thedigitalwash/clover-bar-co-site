# New Hampshire Laws and Rules Governing BYOB Mobile Bartending (Clover Bar Co.)

Scope note: research conducted 2026-10-20. Primary sources are NH RSA text on gc.nh.gov (pages show amendments through 2026). The NH Liquor Commission (NHLC) administrative rules (Liq 100-1100) and several nh.gov / nhstateparks.org pages blocked automated access, so rule-level detail is partly from secondary copies and is flagged. This is not legal advice. Items marked **CONFIRM WITH NHLC** should be verified with the NHLC Division of Enforcement and Licensing before they appear in published copy.

## 1. Can a bartending service legally serve client-purchased alcohol at a private event without a liquor license? Is a cash bar or ticketed event different?

### Takeaway
No NH statute or NHLC page found expressly authorizes or prohibits an unlicensed "labor-only" bartender pouring a host's own alcohol at a private event with no sale. The statutes license *selling* (directly or indirectly) and also reach "giving" or "delivering" liquor to another, so the BYOB model rests on an interpretation (the host, not Clover, is furnishing the alcohol and Clover charges only for labor and supplies) that is widely used in practice but should be confirmed in writing with the NHLC. A cash bar, drink tickets, a ticketed event where admission covers drinks, or any per-drink charge is a sale and requires a license (for example, a one-day license). BYOB is also not allowed at liquor-licensed venues: licensed premises may only serve alcohol sold by the licensee.

### Cited Findings
- RSA 178:1, I: "No person shall manufacture for sale, or sell in any method or manner, directly or indirectly, or distribute by selling, transferring, giving, or delivering to another, or leaving, bartering, or exchanging with another, or offering or agreeing to do the same, in any method or manner, directly or indirectly, or keep for sale any liquor or beverage without first registering to do business with the secretary of state and obtaining a license." Violation is a class B felony per violation. -- [RSA 178:1](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/178/178-1.htm)
- RSA 179:1: "No person shall possess, transport, procure, furnish, or give away any beverage or liquor except such as has been sold under the provisions of this title or legally purchased outside the state." (The client's alcohol must be lawfully purchased, for example from an NH Liquor & Wine Outlet or licensed retailer.) -- [RSA 179:1](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/179/179-1.htm)
- NH defines "Club-bottle" as a business that "maintains a premises that permits the storage of and the consumption of beverage or liquor and that operates with persons who, with or without compensation, furnish support services for the consumption of beverage and liquor to persons who have paid a reasonable fee for such services," and such a business "shall not be issued a license for the sale of liquor." This shows NH law contemplates paid "support services" around guests' own alcohol in at least one setting (a fixed premises), but it is not a mobile/event category. -- [RSA 175:1, XVII](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/175/175-1.htm); club-bottles must register with the Secretary of State and may not "sell beverage or liquor, directly or indirectly, or otherwise replace beverage or liquor ... for a fee or anything of value" -- [RSA 178:1, V](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/178/178-1.htm)
- Licensed premises cannot be BYOB: for on-premises beverage and wine licenses, "No beverages, specialty beverages, or liquor shall be consumed in the licensed areas except those that are sold by the licensee." -- [RSA 178:20, III](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/178/178-20.htm). Same rule for cocktail lounge licenses (with a narrow corkage exception for privately owned table wine with a full-course meal, with commission permission). -- [RSA 178:22, III](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/178/178-22.htm)
- "Liquor" is spirits/wine/malt/cider over 6% ABV; "Beverage" is 0.5% to 6% ABV (most beer). Both are covered by the licensing statutes. -- [RSA 175:1, VIII and XLII](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/175/175-1.htm)
- Industry/vendor sources say mobile bar licensing "depends on the regulations and laws of the specific location" and that a license is required to "sell and serve alcohol to its patrons"; none cite an NH-specific BYOB ruling. (Industry, not law.) -- [Toast, NH mobile bar licenses](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/mobile-bar-business-licenses-and-permits-in-new-hampshire) (page returned 403 on direct fetch; content seen only in search summary); [Bar-a-la-Cart](https://www.baralacart.com/blog/liquor-license-starting-mobile-bar-business)
- Comparison point (not NH law): Colorado regulators describe the host-buys / bartender-paid-for-labor-only model in a published bulletin; no equivalent NH bulletin was found. -- [Colorado SBG Bulletin 18-05](https://sbg.colorado.gov/bulletin-18-05)

### Inferences
- The "indirectly" and "giving, or delivering to another" language in RSA 178:1 is broad. The safest reading for Clover is: the client owns, buys, and furnishes the alcohol; Clover never buys, resells, marks up, stores, or transports alcohol for the client; Clover's invoice is for labor, bar rental, mixers, garnishes, ice, and tools only; and guests are never charged for drinks.
- A cash bar, tip-for-drink setup presented as a price, drink tickets sold to guests, or a ticketed public event that includes drinks would be a "sale ... directly or indirectly" and requires a license (see Section 2).
- Clover's BYOB service cannot operate at a venue that holds its own NH liquor license (restaurants, hotels, licensed function halls, licensed barns). Those venues must sell their own alcohol. BYOB is for unlicensed venues: private homes, unlicensed barns/estates, tents, rentals, etc. This is a useful and accurate FAQ point.
- If Clover ever shops for or picks up alcohol for clients, that likely moves into "transporting/delivering" territory. Copy should say clients buy the alcohol themselves from Clover's shopping list.
- Suggested safe copy framing: "You buy the alcohol, we handle the bar." Avoid statements like "no license needed" or "100% legal everywhere." Prefer: "Our BYOB service is for private events where the host provides the alcohol and guests are never charged for drinks."

### Gaps
- No NHLC FAQ, bulletin, or advisory opinion addressing labor-only BYOB bartending services was found (NHLC pages on nh.gov returned "Access Denied" to automated fetches). **CONFIRM WITH NHLC** (Division of Enforcement and Licensing; NH State Parks lists the Liquor Commission line as (603) 271-3521 -- [NH State Parks SUP page](https://www.nhstateparks.org/fees-reservations/rentals/special-use-permits)). Ideally get written confirmation.
- Liq 100-1100 rule text could not be retrieved (Justia copies were bot-blocked), so rule-level restrictions (if any) on unlicensed service at events are unverified.
- Whether NH Meals and Rentals Tax applies to Clover's service fees (catering-type services) was not researched; Clover should check with NH DRA.

## 2. What licenses or permits exist for events (one-day, caterer, off-premises), and when are they required? Rules on who may pour?

### Takeaway
If alcohol is sold at an event, the main options are a one-day license (separate categories for nonprofits and for-profits) or service by a licensed off-site caterer. The off-site caterer license is built for businesses with a permanent non-residential office, secure liquor storage, and a food component, so it is a poor fit for a BYOB-only bar. "Off-premises" licenses are retail (take-home) licenses and are not event licenses. Under licenses, servers and bartenders must be at least 18.

### Cited Findings
- One-day license for for-profit groups: NHLC "may issue a limited license to any responsible individual representing a for-profit group or organization"; events limited to 500 or fewer patrons; no more than 6 licenses per entity per calendar year; authorizes sale of beverages and liquor on approved premises; requires written approvals from the local fire chief (safety), local health department (sanitary), and police chief (accessibility), and the application must be filed 15 days before the event; under-18s not allowed in service areas unless with parent/guardian/adult spouse; investigators may suspend without warning. -- [RSA 178:22, V(l-a)](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/178/178-22.htm)
- One-day license for voluntary nonprofits: same structure (sale on approved premises; fire/health/police approvals; filed 15 days before). -- [RSA 178:22, V(l)](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/178/178-22.htm)
- Fees: "One day License $100"; "Catering (all) $1,200"; "Catering (off-site only) $840"; "Special License $25". -- [RSA 178:29, I](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/178/178-29.htm). A secondary copy of the NHLC rules says new one-day applicants pay a $25 processing/investigation fee (vs. $100 for most new licenses). -- [Justia copy, Liq 700 (unverified, may be outdated)](https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-hampshire/liq/chapter-liq-700/part-liq-701/section-liq-701-08)
- "Caterer-off site" means (a) a convention center, restaurant, or hotel operator catering private functions off-site, or (b) "an off-site catering service which is a business held out and advertised to the public which has a permanent non-residential business office with securable beverage and liquor storage areas." -- [RSA 175:1, XV](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/175/175-1.htm)
- "Caterer" means a person providing "meals or other food as well as liquor or beverage service to private groups"; "At least 50 percent of a caterer's sales conducted under a license issued by the commission shall be food," with an exemption for caterers with annual food sales of at least $100,000. -- [RSA 175:1, XIV-a](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/175/175-1.htm)
- Off-site caterer licensees may serve "members of a private party contracting for such service" on other licensed premises or on "any public building approved by the commission"; must notify NHLC at least 5 days before each function (date, time, location); new premises must be approved at least 10 days before. -- [RSA 178:22, V(e)(2)](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/178/178-22.htm)
- "Public building" includes tents, gazebos, or other defined outdoor areas if 2 separate toilet facilities are nearby, but "shall not be construed to mean a private residence." "Private group" means persons "present by invitation or reservation" and "in no way ... the general public." -- [RSA 178:22, V(e)(3)](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/178/178-22.htm)
- Caterers must ensure "all state laws are complied with" at every function and file an annual food/alcohol sales certificate (Jan 15 to Feb 15). -- [RSA 178:22, V(e)(4)](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/178/178-22.htm)
- Caterer applicants file Form LTW-34; off-site site approval must be submitted at least ten business days prior. -- [Justia copy, Liq 700 rules (unverified)](https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-hampshire/liq/chapter-liq-700/part-liq-703/section-liq-703-06)
- "Off-premises" means wine and beverage "sold for consumption off the premises" (retail stores). -- [RSA 175:1, L](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/175/175-1.htm); off-premises fees are per-register retail licenses -- [RSA 178:29, II](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/178/178-29.htm)
- Who may pour (licensed context): "An on-premises licensee may employ any person not less than 18 years of age to serve or otherwise handle liquor and beverages while employed as a waiter, waitress, bartender, or hostess"; a person at least 18 must be designated in charge; licensees file felony affidavits for officers/owners. -- [RSA 179:23](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/179/179-23.htm)

### Inferences
- For a client wanting a cash bar or a ticketed/public event, Clover's accurate answer is: "That requires an NH liquor license, so we offer BYOB service for private, invitation-only events. If you want a cash bar, we can point you to a licensed caterer or the one-day license process." (One-day licenses are issued to a "responsible individual representing" a group, not obviously to a vendor; who would hold it is a question for NHLC.)
- The 15-day lead time and fire/health/police sign-offs make one-day licenses impractical for most private parties.
- No statute found sets a minimum age for an unlicensed private-event bartender. Using 21+ bartenders is a defensible policy (industry practice), but should not be described as legally required.

### Gaps
- Whether a one-day license can be held by, or used with, a hired mobile bartending vendor is unclear. **CONFIRM WITH NHLC.**
- RSA 178:21 (beverage and liquor licenses for function rooms) text was not fetched; only a search summary noting private groups contracting for function rooms may be served without food.

## 3. Is alcohol server training (TIPS, NHLC MTS, etc.) required or recommended for private-event bartenders in NH?

### Takeaway
NH has no statewide bartender license or mandatory server certification. The NHLC's free Management Training Seminar (MTS) is mandatory only for licensees or their designated managers. Server training is recommended: NH law reduces penalties for licensees whose staff completed NHLC training in the prior 24 months, and failure to check ID can be evidence of negligence in civil suits.

### Cited Findings
- New retail licensees (or a designated manager) must complete the NHLC Management Training Seminar within 45 days before or after license issuance; NHLC states the course is free. -- [NHLC form L-003 MTS designee](https://www.nh.gov/liquor/enforcement/licensing/documents/l-003-mts-designee.pdf); [NHLC MTS page](https://www.nh.gov/liquor/enforcement/education/mts/) (page blocked to automated fetch; content from search summary)
- Statutory incentive: a licensee that overserves a visibly intoxicated person who then causes serious bodily injury or death faces up to 30 days suspension and a $7,500 fine for a first offense, but the suspension "cannot exceed 10 days if the person in charge, managers, security staff, servers, and clerks have completed liquor commission training within 24 months before the date of violation." -- [RSA 179:5, I-a](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/179/179-5.htm)
- Third-party training vendors state NH does not require statewide server/bartender certification. (Vendor, not law.) -- [360training NH](https://www.360training.com/learn2serve/alcohol/new-hampshire); [iServeSmart](https://iservesmart.com/new-hampshire-bartending-license/); [ServSafe NH guide](https://www.servsafe.com/downloads/pdfs/ssa-guides/newhampshire)
- A TIPS-hosted NH regulations PDF contains contradictory statements about whether TIPS courses are NHLC-approved. -- [gettips.com NH_Regs.PDF](https://gettips.com/PDF/NH_Regs.PDF)
- NHLC offers several free online courses through the state learning portal (lms.nh.gov/liquor/portal), e.g., Restaurant Delivery, Alcohol Consultant, Carrier training; the delivery course covers responsible service, false IDs, and signs of intoxication. -- [NHLC Restaurant Delivery training](https://nh.gov/liquor/enforcement/education/restaurant-delivery.htm); [NHLC Alcohol Consultant course](https://nh.gov/liquor/enforcement/education/alcohol-consultant/index.htm)
- NHLC also lists a one-day training page. -- [NHLC one-day training](https://nh.gov/liquor/enforcement/education/one-day-training.htm) (content not retrieved)

### Inferences
- Accurate copy: "Our bartenders are certified in responsible alcohol service" (naming the actual certification, e.g., TIPS or ServSafe Alcohol) is fine. Avoid "state-licensed bartender" because NH does not issue individual bartender licenses. Avoid "NHLC-certified" unless the bartender actually completed an NHLC course.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether NHLC currently offers a free server-level class open to non-licensees, or which third-party programs NHLC recognizes. **CONFIRM WITH NHLC.**

## 4. Liquor liability insurance norms and venue requirements (certificate of insurance)

### Takeaway
NH law does not require a BYOB bartending service to carry liquor liability insurance (no such statute found), but NH civil law makes anyone who "serves" (including a gift or furnishing) alcohol liable for negligent service to minors or intoxicated people, so coverage is important. Industry norm is $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate with venues named as additional insured. NH State Parks requires $2M per occurrence for alcohol events.

### Cited Findings
- NH dram shop / server liability: "Service of alcoholic beverages" means "any sale, gift, or other furnishing of alcoholic beverages." -- [RSA 507-F:1, IX](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LII/507-F/507-F-1.htm)
- "A defendant who negligently serves alcoholic beverages to a minor or to an intoxicated person is liable for resulting damages"; service is negligent if the defendant knows or a reasonably prudent person would know the person is a minor or intoxicated; "Proof of service of alcoholic beverages to a minor without request for proof of age as required by RSA 179:8 shall be admissible as evidence of negligence." -- [RSA 507-F:4](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LII/507-F/507-F-4.htm)
- Reckless service (actionable even by the drinker) includes actively encouraging intoxicated persons to drink substantial amounts, serving someone 16 or under, service so continuous and excessive it risks alcohol poisoning, and helping a visibly intoxicated person into a vehicle they intend to drive. -- [RSA 507-F:5](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LII/507-F/507-F-5.htm)
- Industry: mobile bartender liquor liability is commonly sold at limits up to $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate, including event-based policies; brokers advertise unlimited additional insureds. (Industry marketing.) -- [Insurance Canopy, mobile bartender](https://www.insurancecanopy.com/alcohol-serving-business-insurance/mobile-bartender)
- Industry/consumer advice: get a certificate of insurance showing liquor liability and share it with the venue in advance; many venues ask to be listed as additional insured. -- [Stacker via KEYT, "Open bar, closed case"](https://keyt.com/stacker-financial-protection/2026/07/24/open-bar-closed-case-do-you-need-liquor-liability-for-events/)
- NH State Parks: "All events that will have alcohol shall require" $2 million per occurrence, $4 million aggregate, $100,000 damage to rented premises, plus "proof of host liquor liability insurance above and beyond the standard insurance requirement," indicated on the certificate. -- [NH State Parks Special Use Permits](https://www.nhstateparks.org/fees-reservations/rentals/special-use-permits); search summary indicates the State must be certificate holder and additional insured -- [GC agenda SUP example](https://media.sos.nh.gov/govcouncil/2025/0326/056%20GC%20Agenda%20032625.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because "service" in RSA 507-F includes "other furnishing," Clover and its bartenders could plausibly be defendants even though the host owns the alcohol. Liquor liability coverage plus ID checks and cut-off policies are the main risk controls.
- Hosts may also want host liquor liability (often an add-on to event or homeowners policies); State Parks explicitly requires it. Suggested FAQ: "Yes, we carry liquor liability insurance and can send your venue a certificate of insurance naming them as additional insured."

### Gaps
- No NH statute found requiring venues or mobile bartenders to carry a specific limit; requirements are contractual (venue-by-venue). Clover should state its actual limits only.

## 5. Alcohol on public property: State Parks, town beaches/parks, and boating

### Takeaway
Alcohol is broadly prohibited on NH seacoast state beaches and on town beaches/parks in Hampton, Rye, Portsmouth, and Laconia (including Weirs Beach), with limited exceptions by special use permit. Seacoast state parks allow alcohol only by special use permit or group reservation, with heavy insurance requirements. On the water, NH's boating-under-the-influence limit is 0.08 BAC (0.02 under 21) for operators. Copy should steer clients to private property, licensed-permit State Park events, or private docks/homes, and never imply beach bars on public beaches.

### Cited Findings
**NH State Parks (Seacoast)**
- State rule for Hampton Beach State Park, Hampton Beach South, North Beach, North Hampton State Beach, Jenness State Beach, Wallis Sands State Park, Odiorne Point State Park, Rye Harbor State Park, and seacoast historic sites: "Possession or consumption of alcoholic beverages shall not be permitted, unless permission has been granted by special use permit or group reservation pursuant to Res 7400." -- [Res 7303.09 (Justia copy; may not be latest)](https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-hampshire/res/chapter-res-7300/part-res-7303/section-res-7303-09)
- NH State Beach Patrol lists alcohol and glass containers as "Not Allowed on the Beach." -- [NH State Parks beach rules](https://www.nhstateparks.org/nh-state-beach-patrol/beach-rules-nh-state-beach-patrol)
- Special use permits: $100 administrative fee; additional $100 expedite fee if submitted less than 30 days before the event; for alcohol, "you must contact the NH State Liquor Commission at (603)271-3521"; Special Use Office 603-271-3556, dncr.stateparkpermits@dncr.nh.gov. -- [NH State Parks Special Use Permits](https://www.nhstateparks.org/fees-reservations/rentals/special-use-permits)
- A 2025 adopted State Parks rule reportedly requires permit applicants to state whether alcohol will be served and, if so, whether by licensed bartender or caterer (seen only in a search summary; PDF could not be retrieved). -- [NH State Parks 2025 adopted text, Ncr 740 (unverified)](https://www.nhstateparks.org/NHStateParks/media/NHStateParks/PDFs/Legislation/6-24-25-2025-50-Adopted-Text-Ncr-740.pdf)
- Some state parks allow alcohol in their listings (e.g., Wellington State Park, glass prohibited) while others ban it (e.g., Silver Lake State Park). -- [Wellington SP facility details](https://newhampshirestateparks.reserveamerica.com/wellington-state-park/r/facilityDetails.do?contractCode=NH&parkId=270091); [Silver Lake SP](https://www.nhstateparks.org/find-parks-trails/silver-lake-state-park)
- Enforcement context: Portsmouth Herald/USA Today Network reported 9,000-plus alcohol violations in one year across Hampton Beach, Jenness Beach, and Wallis Sands. -- [AOL/Seacoast Online, "Can you drink on the beach?"](https://www.aol.com/drink-beach-smoke-rules-seacoast-085821935.html); [AOL, Hampton Beach crackdown](https://www.aol.com/hampton-beach-lifeguards-police-crack-203632542.html)

**Seacoast towns**
- Hampton: town code Chapter 9 makes carrying an open container or drinking alcohol on public property (including beaches and parks) a violation; Chapter 35 (Beaches and Parks) bans alcoholic beverages and glass on public beaches and parks. -- [Hampton Code Ch. 9](https://ecode360.com/14959684); [Hampton Code Ch. 35](https://ecode360.com/14959815)
- Rye: alcohol use "on the beaches and in or upon any and all parking lots or areas easterly of Ocean Boulevard in the Town of Rye is prohibited," fines up to $50 (quoted in news coverage, not verified against Rye's code). -- [AOL/Seacoast Online beach rules](https://www.aol.com/drink-beach-smoke-rules-seacoast-085821935.html)
- North Hampton has a public beaches ordinance (1-77) (text not reviewed). -- [North Hampton Ordinance 1-77](https://www.northhampton-nh.gov/police-department/files/ordinance-1-77-rules-and-regulations-public-beaches)
- Portsmouth: city parks ordinance bars bringing or consuming alcohol in parks; Prescott Park weddings policy says alcohol is prohibited at all times; Four Tree Island page says no alcohol in any city park. -- [Portsmouth Ord. Ch. 8 Parks](https://files.portsmouthnh.gov/files/prescottpark/CityParksOrdinanceChapter8.pdf); [Prescott Park Weddings](https://www.portsmouthnh.gov/prescottpark/weddings); [Four Tree Island](https://www.portsmouthnh.gov/prescottpark/four-tree-island)
- NH Law Library open container guide (for statewide open-container statutes, mostly motor vehicles). -- [NH Law Library, Open Containers](https://courts-state-nh-us.libguides.com/c.php?g=893928&p=6428668)

**Lakes Region**
- Laconia: "Alcohol is prohibited in all City Parks without a special use permit from the Commission for scheduled events." -- [Laconia Parks & Rec Rules (2023)](https://www.laconianh.gov/DocumentCenter/View/10152/Rules-and-Regulations---Updated-for-2023)
- Weirs Beach rules list alcoholic beverages and glass containers as prohibited. -- [Weirs Beach beach rules](https://weirsbeach.com/reasons-to-visit/beach/beach-rules/); [Laconia, Weirs Beach (Endicott Rock Park)](https://www.laconianh.gov/1034/Weirs-Beach-Endicott-Rock-Park)
- Laconia launched New Hampshire's first social district (outdoor drinking zone) in May 2026, operating 12 p.m. to 8 p.m. Thursday through Saturday that summer; outside alcohol is not permitted (drinks must come from participating licensees). -- [WBUR, May 22, 2026](https://www.wbur.org/news/2026/05/22/new-hampshire-outdoor-alcohol-zone-laconia); [Valley News, May 23, 2026](https://vnews.com/2026/05/23/new-hampshire-outdoor-drinking/); [WOKQ](https://wokq.com/laconia-social-districts-memorial-weekend/)

**Boating (Lake Winnipesaukee, etc.)**
- "No person shall operate or attempt to operate a boat while under the influence of intoxicating liquor ... or while such person has an alcohol concentration of 0.08 or more or in the case of persons under the age of 21, 0.02 or more." -- [RSA 265-A:2, II](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXI/265-A/265-A-2.htm)
- A boating-under-the-influence conviction bars the person from operating a boat on NH waters for one year (operating during that period is a misdemeanor); if a child under 16 was aboard, the operator cannot operate until completing a substance use evaluation and impaired-driver program. -- [RSA 265-A:19](https://www.gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXI/265-A/265-A-19.htm)
- Administrative license suspension rules apply to boat operators at 0.08 (0.02 under 21). -- [Saf-C 2801.02 (Justia copy)](https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-hampshire/saf/subtitle-saf-c/chapter-saf-c-2800/part-saf-c-2801/section-saf-c-2801-02)
- Marine Patrol has publicly described a "zero tolerance policy" for operating under the influence (2009 release). -- [Ossipee Lake Alliance, 2009](https://www.ossipeelake.org/2009/06/state-to-increase-enforcement-of-boating-under-the-influence-laws/)

### Inferences
- Lakes Region and Seacoast page copy should frame waterfront service as "private lakefront homes, camps, docks, and venues" rather than public beaches. A dock party on private property is a private event; once guests are on a boat, the operator must stay sober and Clover should not serve the boat operator.
- A wedding at a State Park (e.g., Odiorne Point, Wallis Sands) with alcohol needs a special use permit, NHLC contact, and $2M/$4M insurance with host liquor liability; the 2025 rule language suggests State Parks may expect a "licensed bartender or caterer," which a BYOB unlicensed service may not satisfy. Clover should not advertise State Park beach weddings as BYOB-friendly without confirming with DNCR and NHLC.

### Gaps
- No source found for Ellacoya State Park's specific alcohol rule (Lake Winnipesaukee, Gilford); the seacoast rule does not list it. Check with NH State Parks.
- Meredith, Gilford, and Wolfeboro town beach/park alcohol rules were not found. Check each town's code (ecode360 or town site).
- No NH source found on whether passengers may drink aboard a boat; RSA 265-A covers operators only. Rafting/party-boat rules not researched.
- Res 7300 rule PDFs on nhstateparks.org could not be downloaded; Justia copies may be outdated.

## 6. NH-specific facts for copy: state-run liquor stores, tax-free, outlets near the Seacoast and Lakes Region

### Takeaway
NH is a control state: the NH Liquor Commission runs the NH Liquor & Wine Outlets, which market "tax-free" wine and spirits. Seacoast clients can shop the I-95 Hampton outlets (northbound and southbound), Portsmouth, or Seabrook; Lakes Region clients have outlets in Tilton (I-93 Exit 20) and Gilford, plus Concord (I-93 Exit 17), which NHLC pitched as serving Lakes Region travelers. Exact current Lakes Region addresses should be pulled from the official store locator.

### Cited Findings
- NHLC owns two outlets on I-95 in Hampton, one northbound and one southbound. -- [NHBR](https://www.nhbr.com/new-hampshire-seeks-to-bolster-alcohol-sales-on-i-95/)

## 7. Legal drinking age verification practices, and whether a bartender can refuse service

### Takeaway
NH law prohibits *any person* (not just licensees) from giving alcohol to anyone under 21 or serving anyone visibly intoxicated, so Clover's bartenders must check IDs and refuse service in those cases. Statute lists acceptable IDs for sales. No NH statute requires a server to serve anyone; refusal policies are lawful and are a key liability control.

### Cited Findings
- "No licensee, salesperson, ... nor any other person, shall sell or give away or cause or allow or procure to be sold, delivered, or given away any liquor or beverage to a person under the age of 21, ... or serve an individual who is visibly intoxicated or who a reasonable and prudent person would know is intoxicated." -- [RSA 179:5, I](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/179/179-5.htm)
- Licensees and persons in charge may not allow anyone under 21 to possess or consume alcohol on licensed premises. -- [RSA 179:5, II](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/179/179-5.htm)
- Acceptable proof of age (for sales): NH or other-state/DC/US territory/Canadian province driver's license with DOB, name, address, photo; state-issued ID card; armed services ID; valid passport. Photo ID must match appearance, not be expired, and be free of alteration; ID scanning is permitted but storing personal data is restricted. -- [RSA 179:8](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/179/179-8.htm)
- Minor in possession or intoxicated: violation with a minimum $300 fine ($600 for subsequent); 0.02 BAC is prima facie evidence of intoxication for under-21s. -- [RSA 179:10](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/179/179-10.htm)
- "Minor" means an individual under the age of 21. -- [RSA 175:1, XLIX](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XIII/175/175-1.htm)
- Serving a minor without requesting ID per RSA 179:8 is admissible as evidence of negligence in civil suits. -- [RSA 507-F:4, III](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LII/507-F/507-F-4.htm)
- Responsible-event advice (industry): check IDs and have a plan for guests who appear intoxicated; insurance is not a substitute for responsible event management. -- [Stacker via KEYT](https://keyt.com/stacker-financial-protection/2026/07/24/open-bar-closed-case-do-you-need-liquor-liability-for-events/)

### Inferences
- FAQ-ready: "Yes. We check ID for anyone who appears under 30 (or a stated threshold), and NH law requires us to refuse service to anyone under 21 or anyone visibly intoxicated. Our bartenders have final say at the bar." (The "appears under X" threshold is a business policy, not law.)
- Contracts should state that the bartender may refuse or stop service, and that the host is responsible for not providing alcohol to minors outside the bar.
- Clover should not serve a guest who will operate a boat or car while impaired; RSA 507-F:5 lists helping an intoxicated person into a vehicle as evidence of recklessness.

### Gaps
- No NH statute found that expressly states a "right to refuse service"; the conclusion that refusal is lawful is an inference from the absence of any duty to serve plus the RSA 179:5 duty to refuse minors and intoxicated persons.
- RSA 179:8 is written for "sales"; whether its ID list formally applies to non-sale BYOB service is unclear, but it is the best NH standard to follow.
